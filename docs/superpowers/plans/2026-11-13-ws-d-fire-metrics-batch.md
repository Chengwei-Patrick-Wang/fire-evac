# Workstream D — Fire, Smoke, Metrics and Batch Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Give the crowd sim a fast, explainable smoke-front fire model, then turn runs into door-queue metrics and a many-fire batch whose ranked chokepoints are the demo's hero result.

**Architecture:** `fire/model.py` computes path distances over a 0.5 m walkable grid (closed doors and stairs add distance), turns them into smoke arrival and density, and exposes the `fire/stub.py` API. `fire/metrics.py` reads run/v0 frames; `fire/batch.py` runs one fire per room in a process pool and aggregates batch/v0 with a ranked `top` list.

**Tech Stack:** Python 3.12, numpy 2, shapely 2, heapq, concurrent.futures, matplotlib (debug PNGs). scipy is NOT installed and must not be added.

**Spec:** docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-13-fire-evac-sim-design.md

## Global Constraints

- Contracts (spec §6): meters, floor-local coordinates, `"version"` in every file, `frameInterval` 0.5 s, smoke frame every 2 s; never rename or remove a field (new optional fields are fine); return plain Python numbers (numpy ints break `json.dump`).
- API: `FireModel(building, scenario)` has the exact `fire/stub.py` signatures (`step(t)`, `visibility(floor_id, x, y) -> m`, `untenable(...) -> bool`, `blocked_doors() -> set[str]`, `snapshot(floor_id) -> {origin, cell, cols, rows, d} | None`, `hrr(t) -> kW`). `t` is sim seconds (the clock of `alarmTime`); ignition at `scenario.fire.start`.
- Smoke (spec §7.3): 0.5 m cells; Dijkstra, 8 neighbors; closed door +15 m; stairs +3 m up, +30 m down; `R(t) = ∫ v_s dt`, `v_s = clamp(0.25 + 0.5·Q/1000, 0.25, 1.0)` m/s; `d = 100·min(1, Q/1000)·smokeFactor·e^(−dist/40)·(1 − e^(−(t − T_c)/30))`; `S = min(30, 100/d)`; untenable = fire footprint, or d ≥ 90 for 30 s.
- Fire: `Q = min(α·t², Q_peak)`, α slow/medium/fast/ultra-fast = 0.00293/0.01172/0.04688/0.1876 kW/s²; kitchen fast 1.5 MW, bedroom medium 3 MW, battery ultra-fast 1 MW with smokeFactor 2, trash fast 0.5 MW, liquid instant 1.5 MW; sprinklers cap Q at 0.5 MW.
- Doors: closed doors hold smoke back, open and propped do not; the building's door `state` is overridden by `scenario.doors[id]`. Smoke grid `d` is base64 of a row-major Uint8 (0–100) array from origin = (minx, miny) of the floor's walkable bounds.
- Outputs: `run.metrics.doors[{id, floor, peakQueue, waitPersonSeconds, flow}]` (queue = agents within 2 m of the opening), `heatmaps[{floor, origin, cell, cols, rows, d}]`, P1 `rooms[{id, rset, aset, margin}]`; batch/v0 `{version, id, buildingId, base, runs[{scenarioId, seed, ignition{floor, room}, t90, t100, trapped}], rooms[{id, floor, riskT90, riskTrapped, n}], doors[{id, floor, meanWait, peakQueue, score}], heatmaps[], top[{kind, id, floor, score, why}]}`; `run_many(building, base, ignitions=None, seeds=(1, 2, 3), workers=None)`, rule-based agents only.
- Determinism: the fire has no randomness; batch seeds are `scenario.seed`. Windows and macOS both: pathlib, spawn-safe pools, ASCII-only prints and `why` text.
- Team rules (AGENTS.md): no TDD, `python -m fire.smoke_test` before every push, every constant documented in `fire/README.md` and labeled illustrative, no panic mode, UMass data stays in `data/private/`.

## Review Focus

1. A fire point off the walkable mask (clicked on a wall, or a centroid outside an L-shaped room) must snap into the fire room, never crash or start inside a wall (D2: fires at (−50, −50) and in the wall gap build, source node inside room A).
2. Smoke leaking through walls: 0.2 m wall gaps are thinner than a 0.5 m cell and diagonal steps cut wall corners, so edges need a covered segment, not just two masked ends (D1: a probe 1.4 m across a wall has path distance 8–11 m).
3. A closed door must delay smoke by 15 m, not seal it, and nothing may skip the penalty: a door with no grid node, a node exactly on the passage boundary, or a stair link that starts on a stair-door node (D1: +15.00 ± 0.01 on the 0.5 m and 2.0 m grids; D3: F2 corridor distance ≥ 40 m, finite).
4. Grid size blowing up on a big building: cap 40,000 cells per floor by doubling the cell; Dijkstra on neighbor lists (D1: 300 × 200 m fixture builds in < 10 s with `cols * rows <= 40000`).
5. Batch workers hanging on Windows: spawn context, top-level worker that returns a dict even on failure, building passed once via the pool initializer, wall-clock `timeout_s`, inline path for one worker (D6: 2 workers finish 2 fires inside 240 s, `failed == []`, same `runs` as inline).
